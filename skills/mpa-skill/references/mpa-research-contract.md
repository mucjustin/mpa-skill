# MPA Research Contract

## Applied problem quality

An MPA question must connect a real governance, public-service, organizational, implementation, or policy problem to an answerable analytical question. Separate the practical problem, research question, decision audience, affected stakeholders, and intended contribution.

## Evidence and claims

- Trace important factual claims to a source and page, table, dataset field, interview record, or reproducible calculation.
- Mark interpretation, inference, and recommendation separately from facts.
- Do not fabricate sources, results, respondents, quotations, statistics, rules, or programme requirements.
- Treat course notes and earlier assignments as leads until their original sources are verified.
- Disclose AI assistance as current programme, course, or competition rules require; never present AI-generated text, data, or citations as the student's own.

## Method and causality

Match method to question, access, sample, measurement, and time. Report missingness, exclusions, transformations, diagnostics, uncertainty, and robustness. Do not make a causal claim from descriptive or associational evidence without a defensible identification strategy.

## Public value and feasibility

Identify stakeholders, distributional effects, administrative capacity, costs, legal or institutional constraints, implementation sequence, risks, and evaluation indicators. A recommendation must be actionable by the named decision audience and honest about trade-offs.

## Precedence and ethics

Current programme, university, supervisor, ethics, formatting, and submission rules override generic guidance. For case competition entries, apply [case competition rules](mpa-case-competition.md); the current-year official notice overrides both. Pause when current requirements conflict or cannot be verified. Protect participants, confidential administrative data, and licensed sources.
