# Case Research Engine: evidence-led case study workflow

Use for substantive MPA/public-management case analysis, case-based research design, and case competitions. This is a method protocol, not an additional standalone Skill or mandatory report structure. Follow the current course/programme requirements and the user's requested format. If the user wants only a short classroom answer, apply checks internally and answer briefly. Do not fabricate case facts to fill a template.

## 1. Identify the public-management question before choosing a theory

Separate (a) real-world situation, (b) public-management problem, (c) answerable research question, (d) decision audience, and (e) unit, place, and time boundaries. Offer 2–4 *genuinely different* candidate questions when the research direction is open; do not offer an artificial menu when a well-defined question is provided.

Four nonexclusive lenses may help, but are not a fixed taxonomy:
- **Stakeholder bargaining**: conflicting or aligned interests, bargaining power, strategic moves, negotiated arrangements. Do not invent conflict when evidence only shows cooperation or inaction.
- **Policy implementation and local adaptation**: implementation gap, frontline discretion, capacity, local conditions, feedback and adjustment.
- **Resource activation and governance transformation**: resource control and allocation, organizational relationships, sustainability, changed governance capacity.
- **Development and safeguards**: competing public objectives, equity, legal limits, rights, protection, risk and long-term sustainability.

Compare candidates using: public significance, question specificity, evidence access, method fit, feasible scope, counterevidence opportunities, ethics. Choose one primary question/lens, at most one supporting lens unless the task explicitly requires more; state rejected alternatives and why. Choose theories only *after* the question; justify applicability and observable implications. Use the theory map in mpa-knowledge.md.

## 2. From question to observation: use the evidence protocol

Before treating a proposed mechanism as a finding, create a compact question → claim/hypothesis → observable implication → actor/source → locator → status plan. Read evidence-protocol.md. Separate:
- policy and organizational **statements** (declared goals/interests);
- **observed conduct** (decisions, implementation records, contracts, actual resource allocation);
- **outcomes** (observable results, beneficiaries, distribution and unintended effects).
Do not infer conduct from declarations or infer motives from outcomes alone. If sources are unavailable, output a collection plan, not invented interview quotes, participants, statistics, records, or dates.

## 3. Stakeholders are dynamic, not a fixed cast

Identify only context-relevant actors: decision makers, frontline staff, intermediary organizations, affected citizens, businesses, community groups. For each actor, track role, interests (reported/inferred separately), power/resources, constraints, observable choices, and evidence IDs. Mark who is absent from available sources.

Reconstruct phases only when supported: initiation, implementation/interaction, adjustment/outcome (or the case's actual phases). Conflict, agreement, nonparticipation, policy resistance and stable routines are all possible; do not force a three-act conflict. Identify changing alliances and distributional impacts with dated evidence.

## 4. Process trace and test mechanisms

Build a case timeline with dated or explicitly undated events; each material event carries evidence ID(s). Proposed explanation:
initial condition → trigger/event → actors' observed action → intermediate mechanism → observed outcome.
For each arrow state what evidence would support it, which evidence does support it, and what remains unobserved. Distinguish chronology and plausible interpretation from a tested causal mechanism.

When explanations are genuinely plausible, state at least one rival (e.g., external funding, selection effects, baseline advantages, regulations, organizational incentives) and identify evidence discriminating between them. Search for negative cases, contradictory testimony, omitted groups, changed policy timing, and alternative sequences. Do not claim a mechanism is established merely because the outcome followed an intervention.

## 5. Research gates and deliverables

- **Design gate**: question, scope, stakeholders, lens, theory fit and evidence-access plan explicit. If insufficient, deliver a provisional design with TODO; do not masquerade as completed research.
- **Evidence gate**: critical assertions mapped to source locators; disputed/unverified/missing items stay visibly qualified. Without sufficient evidence, reduce claim strength and generate next collection tasks.
- **Mechanism gate**: chronology supported, intermediate links inspected, plausible rivals considered, alternative explanations not silently discarded.
- **Recommendation gate**: proposed intervention addresses a documented problem, identifies owner, capacity, legal/ethical constraints, costs, affected groups, monitoring, and limits to transfer.

Default lightweight outputs when needed: (1) lens diagnosis, (2) actor–action–evidence matrix, (3) evidence gap/collection plan, (4) event timeline, (5) mechanism vs rival explanations. Produce only the smallest subset required for the user's task. Keep [证据] / [推断] / [建议] separate. Share no identifiable fieldwork records in public artifacts.
