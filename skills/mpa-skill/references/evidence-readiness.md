# Evidence readiness for libraries and supplied corpora

Use when a task relies on an existing literature library, source catalog or supplied research corpus. Inspect the actual files or authorized interface; do not assume a path, schema, integration, or need to create a workbench registry.

## Find and check relevant sources

- Identify the current catalog and its coverage. Combine current partitions by stable source identity; count canonical sources once and retain duplicate aliases as locators. Historical summaries do not establish the current total.
- Use categories, tags, abstracts and reading notes as discovery hints. Keep identity, access, extractable text, actual reading scope and claim support separate. A linked attachment is not proof of readable text; an empty evidence field is unknown. Key-section reading does not establish a full-paper audit.
- Before citing method evidence, distinguish a method review, an empirical application and a paper that merely mentions the method. Compare catalog labels with the source's design and analysis. Report both sides of a material conflict in the answer, even if a separate data gap already blocks feasibility. If the passage is insufficient, leave the role unresolved. Flag catalog errors without silently changing the library.
- Check citation identity and the actual passage, page or section. For OCR, compare material quotations, formulas and numbers with the rendered original. A quotation matched in an extracted packet establishes consistency with that packet only.
- Distinguish what authors report from independently corroborated facts. A paper's causal claim or successful local case does not by itself establish causality or transfer to the target setting. Identify rival explanations and target-context checks when they affect the requested claim.

## Check project feasibility separately

Literature can justify concepts and candidate methods; it does not establish access to project data. For quantitative or longitudinal designs, inspect permission, codebooks, units, time points, matching identifiers, measurement comparability, sample size, missingness and attrition before calling the design feasible. For fieldwork, check site access, sampling and consent. If unavailable, keep the design provisional and identify the smallest next check.

For a substantial task, a compact record may help: claim or decision → source and role → checked locator and reading scope → support and remaining limit → next check. A short answer can use prose. Do not convert collection size or reading-status tags into a readiness score.

## Optional evidence workbench

When persistence is requested or already authorized, use the [bundled CLI guide](evidence-workbench-usage.md) to record actual sources, claims and quotations. Do not turn a read-only library assessment into an import. Distinguish your source assessment from the registry's human review status: never mark evidence `verified` on the model's own judgement. Preserve the explicit researcher review gate and the requested workspace and persistence boundaries.
